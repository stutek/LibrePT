# LibrePT Changelog

Shipped changes, newest first. Backlog and open questions live in [TODO.md](TODO.md); a completed
backlog item graduates here as soon as it has shipped and passed the gate, leaving a one-line stub
behind so its `§N.M` number keeps resolving — those numbers are cited from `src/`, `tests/` and
`docs/`, so they are identifiers, not just headings.

Format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com): grouped into **Added**, **Changed**, **Fixed**.

## 2026-08-25 — The demo's own front door

### Changed
- **A client's details open the app straight from the message** — asked as "does a PWA import really need saving the file and then opening it?". It did: save the attachment, open LibrePT, open the menu, choose Review a client's file, find it in the picker. Now the trainer taps Share on the attachment and picks LibrePT, or taps the file itself, and lands in the review with her details already on screen. Nothing is imported without a person reading it — that has not changed, and will not. The menu's file picker stays, because iPhones have neither mechanism. The demo shows the new way: a drawn screenshot of the message with the attachment under it, and tapping it opens the app exactly as the real thing does.
- **The demo card is put away, not shut** — reported as "exiting does not allow for return to demo". The corner of the card held a ✕ that ended the walkthrough outright: the most final thing you can do, wearing the glyph that everywhere else means "close this box", so 49 steps in the whole thing was one casual tap from gone. It now parks the guide the way the message drawer parks itself — a bar saying which step of how many, on whose phone, and tapping it anywhere brings the card back. The guide keeps watching while it is parked, so doing the step yourself with the card out of the way brings you back to a guide that moved on with you. Ending the demo is offered from the parked bar, where the ✕ sits next to the word DEMO and means what it looks like.
- **A reload no longer throws away a half-filled form** — asked for after a page reload emptied the intake form; measured first, and it took the name, the email, the phone number and both paragraphs with it. That form is the longest thing anyone fills in on their own phone and there is no second copy of it anywhere. What is typed now survives a reload and dies when the tab closes, which meant sharpening what the page promises: it used to say "nothing is saved on this phone", and now says nothing leaves the phone and what you type is kept only until you close the tab. The consent tick is the one thing that does not come back — agreement is given, not restored. The trainer's client form does the same, remembering per client, and Cancel still means throw it away.
- **Every card the demo shows is the same card** — asked as "poenoti vse demo kartice, da bodo enotne". A chapter opening, a message arriving on somebody's phone, something that happens on paper, and the guide telling you that you have wandered off were four things to read in three different shapes — the last one was not even a card, just the instruction line overwritten in place. They are one family now, each kind declaring only what makes it different: a message is set as the quotation it is, paper keeps its texture, and the guide's own card says where you are in the guide's words rather than repeating an instruction about a control that is no longer on screen. A kind nobody drew used to produce an unstyled box in silence; now it produces nothing, and the build refuses a demo script that names one.
- **The demo counts one story across both phones** — asked as "why is the counter on Ana's phone 1/10, why does it not carry on the way it has until now". It went step 10 of 41, then step 1 of 8 when the story handed the browser to the client's page, then step 19 of 41 on the way back: three numberings for one story, because the counter counted whichever list was being played and her chapter is a separate one, run in its own boot on a stranger's phone. A step now knows its place in the story rather than in the run it happens to be in, so the crossing reads 10 of 49 → 11 of 49. The buttons still obey the run they are in — otherwise her page would be waiting for step 49 to finish — and a link to one chapter opens at that chapter's place in the story, which is what says you are joining part-way.
- **The demo card follows the app** — do what a step asks, by tapping the control yourself or through "Show me", and the card moves to the next step instead of sitting there repeating an instruction you have just carried out. Reported three ways in one session: a modal closed by hand and the card still asking for it, and twice as "Show me does not fill the form" when the filling was the next step. A card that arrives already true — a page of narration, or a step the previous screen answers — still waits for Next, so the story cannot race past two steps at once, and the last step always waits, because ending the demo is a decision.
- **Going exploring mid-demo is met with two buttons, not a broken guide** — open some other part of the app and the card says you have left the demo's place, and offers "Back to the demo" or "Stop the demo". Scrolling away, or opening a menu over the control, is not leaving: the guide handles both itself.
- **The demo-mode notice is the first thing in the message area** — it led the stored messages, but the feed puts work the trainer owes above FYI, and the demo data generates exactly that work, so the card that says "none of this is real" sat below sample bookings and pending sessions. It now leads everything. Every other item is a claim about the trainer's own gym, and reading one before knowing the data is a fiction is reading it wrong — and this card is also the collapsed drawer's summary line and the only way back to the guided demo and the cleanup screen.

- **The invitation text names the trainer and carries the privacy notice** — a client who has met their trainer once gets a text starting with that trainer's name and the app's, then the link, then "What happens to your data" with the notice's own address. It used to open with "Fill in your details for our training", which from an unknown number is indistinguishable from a phishing message, and said nothing about data until the person was already filling the form in. The notice link is the same shipped page the consent letter uses, in the language the invitation opens in.

### Fixed
- **The Slovenian app stops slipping into English** — reported as "gumb cancel se pojavi na slovenski izvedbi". Not a missing translation: the dictionaries match exactly, and the English was written into the markup. Twenty-seven elements already carried the key for their own text and nothing was reading it, so the whole session editor, the invite button and the clipboard's plan menu shipped English in every language — that mechanism now works, along with two siblings for placeholder text and for what a screen reader is told about an icon button. The client dialog the report came from is fully translated, mostly with keys the dictionary had been holding all along. A ratchet now keeps the remaining count from rising while the sweep continues.
- **The demo can be finished in Slovenian** — reported from a Slovenian run as "step 16 of 49 could not be completed": the step where Ana sends her details asked the page to say "Shared", and the Slovenian page says "Deljeno", so the story stopped dead there for every Slovenian viewer while passing every test we had. Four steps were reading the app's own translated words back to it; they now ask for facts the language cannot change — which channel the invitation chose, whether the file was shared, which kind of badge the editor drew. And what the demo types is content, so it is translated too: Ana used to write up her shoulder in English on a Slovenian phone, and now writes "rama, pred dvema letoma". A check refuses any demo step that matches on words the app never says in that language.
- **"Show me" always carries the demo on** — reported as "show me behavior is inconsistent, should it advance always or never?", and it was neither: on a step whose screen the previous one had already produced, being shown it left the card sitting there, while the identical tap one step earlier moved on. Nothing on screen told the two apart. Two rules written three days apart had come to disagree — one said Show me never moves the guide, the other said the card follows the app whenever a step is done in front of you — and Show me is a step done in front of you. Now there is one rule for both hands: a step completed while you are watching carries the card on, whoever did it. The last step still waits, because ending the demo is a decision, and a step you walked back to is re-explained rather than skipped past.
- **The demo's card stops standing on the control it is pointing at** — walked again at iPhone sizes after "popravi čudno postavljene kartice". On an iPhone SE the guide's card took 63% of the screen at the very first step of the story, hung off the bottom edge at another, and at four steps across the two phones it covered the session card its own highlight was ringing. The card is now bounded and its prose scrolls inside, so the guide always has an end of the screen to move to — and where a control sits in the middle, where neither end helps, the guide scrolls the control out from under itself instead, once, and never while it is demonstrating.
- **The demo's last chapter opens on the right screen** — found by walking all 49 steps by hand at full speed, which is not what the automated walk does. Three steps into the evening the guide greyed out every button for five seconds and then told the trainer to go back to the sessions board, while standing on it. The chapter's opening card is where the story says which screen the chapter happens on, and merging that card into the first step — so nobody has to tap Continue on a paragraph — kept the words and dropped the screen. So the evening began wherever the gym had left the app: deep inside the plan editor. The other three chapters were fine by luck. Every chapter now has to say where it starts, and the build fails if one stops saying it.
- **The demo's story card is readable on every theme** — reported as "the 2/8 card is hard to read", and measured at 2.38:1 against the 4.5:1 a paragraph needs. The card asked the stylesheet for four colours this app has never defined, so each one silently fell back to the light-theme value typed beside it — right on Daylight by coincidence, dark-on-dark on the other four palettes, and the story's own handover link forces one of those on the client's phone. Fixing the card was four lines; the rest of the day went into the check that now fails the build for any colour nobody defines, which found 38 more across twelve files: feed cards that had a border and no surface, seven text colours that quietly did nothing, and the backup warning's frozen amber that ignored the theme you picked. One of them was the app's own muted plum on the Blossom theme, under the readability bar for every muted line in the app, not just this card.
- **The rings a demo tap leaves stay on the screen that was tapped** — reported as "the ripple effect is sometimes too late, the application already loads the new view when the effect fires". The click is what changes the screen, and the mark was only 160ms into a set of rings that take most of a second when it fired: the two trailing rings had not yet been sent, and the whole thing played out over a view it never touched, reading as a tap on the screen that had just arrived. The hand now spends a whole ring on the control before the app is told anything. Two things came out with it: the delayed rings had been painted at full size while they waited their turn — one hard blob at the contact point, then a snap back to a third of the size before anything rippled — and their fading, which is what keeps the group from looking like a target reticle, had never actually shown.
- **Walking back through the demo puts the screen each card describes back on** — reported as "going back from step 7 to step 4 does not clear/update the intake address/number". It was worse than stale: those steps showed an empty contact box under cards asking for a number to be typed over another number. Reopening the invite dialog empties its field, correctly, and the guide stopped rebuilding there because the step's own control was reachable again. Ground now means what the step before left on screen, and the last few taps are put back with it.
- **The guide stopped counting steps nobody had done, and the intake modal stopped swallowing the app** — reported as "send intake link does not count the steps right, back button keeps the app stuck in the modal". A modal makes the rest of the page inert, but the buttons behind it keep their boxes, and the step that closes the invite dialog only claimed "the register button is visible" — true from the moment the dialog opened. So it was finished before it happened, the guide lit Next, and everything after it ran over a modal nobody had closed, with no way back out of it. The guide now reads reachable rather than painted, that step claims what it is about, and rebuilding a step's ground closes a modal the step is not in.
- **The demo card is out of the modal, and behaves itself in the ones it cannot leave** — reported four ways over a day: "hijacking the demo step card", "really long scroll bars" down the modal, "does not display the demo card anymore, so I can't click show me or next or back", and finally "still trapped in the modal view and covers the interface". The card has to move into an open dialog to stay tappable at all — everything outside a modal is dead to a tap — and a dialog clips what hangs off its edges, which is what trapped it. That clipping is now lifted while the guide is in there, so the card draws at the bottom of the screen where it always sits. A dialog that needs its own scrolling keeps the card docked inside it instead: the new-client form is taller than a phone, and a form that cannot scroll is worse to hand someone than a card in the way.
- **Walking back and forth across the intake-link steps works** — reported as "back and forth for demo steps surrounding sending intake link don't work". Going back out of the invite dialog closes it, which is right; walking forward again then marched through four steps whose controls were inside that closed dialog, ticking each one off over a screen where none of it was happening. A step is now only ready to be asked for when its own control is actually there, so walking forward re-opens what it needs.
- **The guide clears what the app left on top of a step before showing it** — reported as "manually open menu and click show me -> observe menu is not closed (no state enforcement)". A dropped-down menu is not modal and nothing looks broken; it just covers, and a demonstration under one is a hand tapping something the viewer cannot see. Whether a control is reachable is now asked at its own centre rather than from its rectangle, and an open menu is closed through the control that opened it.
- **Asking to be shown a step twice no longer blinks what it opened** — reported as "show me on step 3/41 seems to loop". That step opens the invite dialog, which puts the button it tapped out of reach behind that dialog; being shown it again meant closing the dialog, tapping the button and opening it afresh, taking anything typed in the meantime with it. A step that is done and whose control its own success removed has nothing left to show, so nothing happens.
- **Asking to be shown a step that opened a dialog no longer reports a failure** — the step's own success puts its control behind the modal it opened, so a second "Show me" could not find the button it had just pressed. It rebuilds and demonstrates again, like every other step.
- **Asking to be shown a step again leaves the app where the step leaves it** — reported as "step 3 does not ensure menu closed". Being shown a step a second time rebuilds what the first time used up, which for the step that opens the client register means re-opening the ☰ menu; the tap that would have closed it again was skipped as redundant, so the menu was left covering the next step's control. A step replayed onto rebuilt ground is performed for real.
- **"Show me around" starts the story, not the four-tap tour it replaced** — reported as "the message in the notification area starts the old 1/4 demo, not the new 1/31", and the splash's offer did the same, because both build their link from one function and that function still asked for the old script. Nothing looked wrong in the code: the story runs on the same guided panel, so the only symptom was the counter saying 1 of 4 in front of whoever accepted the invitation. The tests now name the script the button must start instead of checking that it starts something.

## 2026-08-21 — The live session stops being one file

### Changed
- **The icon font is 7KB instead of 252KB** (TODO §12.6) — the app shipped two whole Font Awesome faces, ~1900 glyphs, to draw the 72 it uses; it now ships subsets of exactly those 72. That is a quarter of a megabyte off the **first** load, which in a basement gym is the load this app is designed around. Two subsets rather than one merged font, and that was measured rather than assumed: merging made two brand icons silently draw something else, because the faces share 96 codepoints and a merge forces a winner per codepoint. The subsets are Modified Versions under the font licence, so they are renamed (`LibrePT Icons`, `LibrePT Icons Brands`) and carry the copyright and licence in their own name tables. **The check came before the cut**: a name-level gate cannot see a glyph that is missing from a subset, so every icon is now drawn through the shipped stylesheet, reduced to a 16×16 grid and compared against a recorded baseline — a shape comparison, because re-encoding a font legitimately moves antialiasing while a wrong glyph moves half the grid.
- **The icon check now counts icons declared in JavaScript, not only in markup** (TODO §12.6) — `overlayIcon: "fa-solid fa-slash"` in a status map, and `isActive ? "fa-circle-check" : "fa-feather"` on the deck's cards, are both how this app picks a glyph, and the check only ever read `class="…"` attributes. Four visible icons went missing the first time the font was subset for exactly that reason — the header's demo badge and its offline-cloud mark, and the circuit cards' Too Easy / Too Hard glyphs — because the check said everything shipped while thirteen icons it had never counted were absent from the font. All three shapes are read now, each anchored (the attribute, a style token, or a string that is nothing but icon names), and **comments are stripped first**: widening the scan immediately made the tool read its own explanation of a Pro-only glyph as a usage of it, which is the false positive the original narrow rule existed to avoid.

### Fixed
- **Crossing to the client's phone is the guide's own Next** — reported as "why is Open Ana's phone a different button from Next, and why does Next skip the intake form?". It skipped it because advancing the guide and going to the other phone were two different actions sitting side by side, and the plain-looking one did the wrong thing. Next now wears the words for what it does — "Open Ana's phone", then "Back to your own phone" on the way back — and the story is one walk over two phones. The way back resumes the trainer's run **by step**, so the counter picks up at 6 of 32 rather than restarting a story the viewer is five steps into.
- **Asking to be shown again starts from where the step starts** — reported as "multiple clicks on Show me should reset the state first". The step that opens the client register taps a row inside the menu, and succeeding closes that menu — so a second Show me looked for a row that was no longer there and told the trainer the step had failed when it had worked. A step now declares what it needs on screen, and the guide rebuilds exactly that before demonstrating, the same way it does when a card arrives on an app that has drifted.
- **The card lost its own Continue button, so step 4 is not a dead end** — reported as "next button is disabled at 4 of 31". A card that is only read had its own way on, sitting beside the guide's greyed-out Next: two buttons, one dead, and the live one not the one you would reach for. There is one way on everywhere in the story now.
- **Whose phone you are looking at is written beside the step number** — it used to be a black tag over the app's own header, where it went unnoticed and read as one of the app's own controls. It says "Your phone" and "Ana's phone", in the same glance as "step 4 of 31". The client's page also opens in a different theme, so the two phones do not look alike at all.
- **Show me demonstrates a step; only Next moves the guide on** — reported as "sometimes Show me advances the demo step, sometimes not". It did: asking to be shown advanced, doing it yourself did not, and the last step was a third case again. Three rules for one button reads as a bug from the gym floor whichever way it falls that day, so there is one rule now, and it costs a tap nobody has to think about. It also removed a step that could enter itself in a circle — a step whose ground was rebuilt could satisfy the next one, which advanced into it, which rebuilt again; on screen that was the flicker reported at story step 3.
- **The card stopped bouncing up and down the screen** — reported at story step 4 of 31. The guide moves its panel out of the way of whatever the current step points at, and that step points at the Continue button on the panel itself: moving the panel moved the target with it, so the answer flipped four times a second. A control on the card is never covered by the card, and the guide now says so.
- **A reload comes back on the step it left** — reported as a reload part-way through the story landing on somebody else's first card. Each step now names itself in the address, so the address is enough to come back to, or to send to a colleague half way through. It replaces the address rather than pushing it: Back belongs to the app, and a 31-entry history of one demo would bury it. A link naming a step that no longer exists starts the story from the top rather than showing an error — a stale link is a wrong URL, not a fault to report.
- **The intake link stops appearing beside a send that worked** — a share sheet, or "Link copied" on the button, already says what happened; a text field arriving next to it read as the send having failed and offered a second thing to do for something already done. The field is now the fallback for the case where neither route was available, which is the only case it was ever for.
- **The demo is one card now, numbered from the first thing you read** (TODO §30.5) — reported as "the first card has no 1/31, and the second one is numbered wrong and has no Back". It was two boxes: the story's words floated in the middle of the screen with no number and no way back, while the guide's own panel sat below saying "Step 1 of 31" about what looked like the second card. They are one card: the step count, the story, the line saying what to do, and Back / Show me / Next, in that order. The words scroll inside it rather than growing the card, because the guide has to be able to move itself off the control it is pointing at.
- **The ring stopped skipping controls that live in the app's top bar** — the very first step, "open the menu", had nothing marked on screen at all. The rule that hides the ring for a control scrolled up behind the header was catching the header's own ☰ button, which is not scrolled anywhere. Whether a control lives in the header now decides it, not where it happens to sit.
- **Steps that ask you to tap something say what to look for and where** — "Open the menu" became "Tap the ☰ button — three stacked lines, in the top right corner of the screen, in the dark bar with the app's name". A demo that names a control the reader cannot find has stopped demonstrating anything.
- **The guide could be left inside a dialog it had just closed** — a closed dialog draws nothing, so the panel and everything in it measured as invisible for a quarter of a second. Harmless while the panel held only text; once the story moved into it, a step that ends when the card goes away declared itself finished the instant it started, and the story skipped two steps in a blink.
- **The icon render check became a test instead of a command** (TODO §12.6) — it asserts something that has to stay true (every icon draws, and draws the shape it drew before), and a check that only runs when somebody remembers reports on whichever font was current the last time somebody remembered. Recording the baseline stays a command, because that is a deliberate act taken with a regenerated font. The failure now names the icons and how far they moved, and says how to re-record.
- **The guided demo stopped closing the things it was pointing at** (TODO §30.5) — reported as "step 2 of 31 … this step didn't complete": the ☰ menu closes on any click outside it, and *Show me* is outside it, so the guide dismissed the menu holding the item its next step was about. Every "tap outside closes me" rule in this app means *outside the thing you are working on*, and the guide floats over the app — so the plan editor, both menus and the story card's own dismissal now ask one shared question instead of four private ones. Pinned by a test about the TAP: the demo's own walk missed all three cases, because it taps Show me at moments where the closure happens to do no harm.
- **A story card is now part of the step it introduces** (TODO §30.5) — reported as "Show me does nothing" on the opening step. It did something: it spent three seconds walking a pointer to the Continue button already under the reader's thumb, which from outside is a guide that has stopped working. Cards ride on the next real step now — read it, then do the thing it is about, and the first tap takes it away — so the story went from 35 steps to 31, every one of them something a person does. A card with nowhere to ride stays a step and hides Show me, because offering to demonstrate a button in front of you is worse than offering nothing.
- **The story's second caption is gone from on top of the guide's buttons** (TODO §30.5) — a bar fixed to the bottom of the screen, carrying the caption the guide's own panel already shows, drawn over its buttons. It was written when the story played itself and nothing else showed captions, and has had no reason to exist since the story became guided. The card also moved to the upper half of the screen, where it cannot cover the panel.
- **The guided demo stopped asking for a screen the trainer was already past** (TODO §30.4) — reported with a screenshot: "step 1 of 4, open the group session" over an already-open clipboard, because this app restores a live session on its own and the first step was satisfied before anyone read it. The guide now moves past a step that was already done when it arrived. **Not** by rebuilding the screen, which was the first attempt and turned the guide against the trainer — tapping the session card themselves opened the clipboard and the guide navigated back out of it, caught by a test within the minute — and never on Back, where a step returned to stays put.
- **The guide's highlight keeps up with the control** (TODO §30.4) — a card expanding under the ring fires no scroll and no window resize, so the highlight sat on the old geometry for up to a quarter of a second, which on screen reads as it sliding after the fact. It follows the target's own shape now.
- **Too Easy / Too Hard / Notes look the same wherever they are** (TODO §30.4) — grey and 32px tall inside a circuit, coloured and 40px on a standalone card, for the same tap with the same meaning: a trainer learned one vocabulary at the start of a session and met another halfway through. The circuit rows now carry the deck's own classes, so one place decides the look, and the 32px target — under what this app promises everywhere else — is gone with it.
- **The demo's own welcome card had an invisible icon** — `fa-sparkles` is a Font Awesome **Pro** glyph and has never been in the free set this app ships, so that card has been rendering a gap where its icon should be since it was written. Found by the widened icon check above, not by looking.

### Added
- **The story shows the trainer opening what Ana sent, and her landing in the register** — the step the whole first chapter is for, and the one the demo skipped. It was filed as unscriptable: opening a file needs the operating system's picker, which no page can drive, and the alternative on the table was a "hand me a submission" hook inside the shipped app — which the review dialog refuses on purpose, because that hook would exist in everybody's build for the demo's convenience. **The seam moved instead of the refusal**: the demo engine now puts a real file on the real input and fires the same event the picker fires, so the app reads it, refuses what it does not recognise, looks for a client it might already be, and saves — all unchanged. What a viewer sees is the trust boundary doing its job: nothing she sent touched the register until the trainer read it and tapped Add to my clients.
- **The story onboards all three friends, by all three routes** — asked for as "onboard two clients this way to show both channels, and the third one manually". Ana's number gets a text message, Maja's address gets an email — typed over the same one box, which is what a trainer does when the next person is standing there and what shows the app reading which kind of contact it was given — and Nik, who is right there reading his details out, is added by hand in three taps. The point the chapter now makes is that the link is not the only way in, and that the trainer typed four words for three clients.
- **Ana's half of the story is played end to end, sending included** — asked for as "Show me should fill in all the fields and also send, with a mock of receiving the link and of sharing the file". Her chapter now opens with the message that actually lands on her phone — the trainer's own words with the link in them — the guide fills the form and taps Send on the real button, and the file's arrival is narrated where it happens, in the trainer's messaging app. **Only the last inch is mocked**: the form, the validation and the file are the real page doing the real thing, but a demo may not drop a `.librept-signup` file into the Downloads folder of everyone who watches. The message steps are drawn as messages, never as a screen of this app — the same rule the paper-form steps follow.
- **The client's page wears the theme its link names**, so the two phones in the story do not look alike. The theme reached `<html>` before paint but never `<body>`, which kept the light class, because the only way to apply one also **wrote** it — and nothing may be written on a stranger's phone. Applying without persisting fixed both halves at once.
- **The intake page says who the link came from** — asked as "how would she know this is not phishing?". It asked a stranger for their health details while identifying nobody, which is exactly the shape of a phishing attempt. The trainer's name and number now travel in the link's fragment — never sent to a server, never logged, never passed on in a `Referer` — and the page shows them above the form with the one thing that is actually true beside them: **this page sends nothing by itself**; the answers become a file on that phone and the person chooses who to share it with. It **cannot prove** who sent the link, and says so rather than implying otherwise: with no server there is nothing to check identity against. What it gives the reader is something to compare — the name in the message, the name on the page, and the person they just spoke to.
- **The invitation is signed with the trainer's name and number** — asked for as "can it already carry a contact to save in the phone?". Neither `sms:` nor `mailto:` can attach a contact card, but a number written in the message is tappable on both phones and reaches the address book in two taps. Nothing is added when the trainer has not filled their details in, because a signature reading "— ," is worse than none.
- **The consent a client ticks now says what they are agreeing to** — it read "I agree to my trainer storing and using these details to plan and log my training", while the record it wrote was the same full-processing consent a signed paper form produces, and the optional backup copy in the trainer's own Google Drive was disclosed only in the two documents linked below it. Informed consent rests on full disclosure or it is void, and the line beside the box is the only part most people read. It now names where the details live, the backup, that no other service receives them, and the right to withdraw. **The form version is deliberately unchanged**: the promises did not change, the sentence stopped under-stating them, and a bump would ask everyone who already signed to sign again.
- **The client's own page says "share", like everywhere else** — its buttons said "Send to my trainer" and "Save the file to send yourself", which is a different word for the one thing this app does with a file. Sharing is what the button does and what the phone calls it.
- **The intake link can be addressed to the person who just asked** (TODO §26.3 step 2) — until now sending it meant the phone's share sheet, which is fine on a phone and nothing at all on a desktop. Now there is one field that takes either a phone number or an email address: the app reads which it is from what was typed, and opens the trainer's own messages or mail app with the invitation already written. **It never sends anything itself** — the message leaves from the trainer's own number or address and lands in their own sent items, so no carrier and no mail server is involved. ## 2026-08-17 — Clients introduce themselves; a walkthrough that drives the real app

### Added
- **A changed session offers to tell the clients** (TODO §1.6) — moving a session's time or room asks once whether the people already invited should get the new details, names which detail moved, and on yes reopens the ordinary invite dialog for exactly those clients. Four triggers and no more: the slot, the room, the session's **kind** (leg strength becoming cardio changes how a client eats and what they bring — derived from the modalities its routine prescribes, never stored), and the **invitee list**, since a one-to-one that becomes a group of five is a different offer. **A rename or a same-kind plan edit asks nothing**: a prompt that fires when a trainer fixes a typo is one that gets dismissed on the change that mattered. Only clients who were invited AND are still participants are offered — quietly turning a time change into someone's first invitation is a different feature. Saying no sends nothing and leaves no dialog behind. Resending refreshes `sentAt` and keeps the answer already given, which stays legible as "answered before this went out" precisely because both are UTC instants.
- **Invitations can expire** (TODO §1.6) — the trainer sets how many hours before a session replies close, and the cutoff **travels in the invite** as an absolute instant, because the client's device knows only what the invite told it. Past it, the reply page closes and points at the one route that still works: messaging the trainer. Three decisions: **derived, never stored** (nothing runs at the cutoff, so a stored `expired` would only become true if the app happened to be open); **advisory, and worded that way** (two devices, two clocks, no server — nothing recalls a message in flight, and a late answer that arrives is still recorded, which is why there is no "late" flag and only a response time); and **0 means "no deadline", which is not the same as unset**, so turning expiry off does not get it reinstated by a default. Exactly at the cutoff is still open — "four hours before" reads as "up to", and refusing that second would be a coin toss on clock skew.
- **An RSVP has somewhere to live, and staging is enforced for the first time** (TODO §1.6/§18.4) — the trainer taps the link a client's reply carried and the answer lands in their own store. Each maintainer ruling changed the shape:
  - **"Invites should host the RSVP status, sessions should host attendees list (by reference only for easier anonymization)"** — `invites` is its own collection, and an RSVP is a fact about a message that was sent rather than a property of a person (who answers differently per session) or of a session (which has no single answer). `sessions.participants` stays authoritative, so ingestion **never touches it**: a "no" is an answer, not a withdrawal. The record holds two ids and the facts about the message — no name, no contact — which is what makes anonymisation cheap: erasing a client leaves every invitation structurally valid with nothing in it to redact.
  - **"Not all attendees need an invitation, some will be added manually"** — an attendee with no invitation is the ordinary case, so nothing reads an absent invitation as "not coming".
  - **"No need for a special mark for late responses, just record response time"** — `answeredAt` and `sentAt` are both UTC instants, so the difference is correct across any pair of timezones. The convention is written down now: **instants are UTC, calendar dates are local** — "at" versus "on" — with the consent date as the case that proves it, since `toISOString()` there once filed a 00:30 signature as the previous day.
  - **"Not modifying [schema 4] would actually test our rollout plans"** — and it did. Declaring the first preview-only collection found that **nothing enforced staging**: the fan-out wrote every projected record into every live store, and the backup file walked the projector table, which knows nothing about schemas. A preview-only shape would have landed in `schema4` and in every backup, undeclared — the exact failure staging exists to prevent, invisible until someone tried it. A record now reaches only stores whose schema declares its collection, and a backup carries the collections of the schema it is written at. **The cost is accepted and visible**: a restore replaces preview-only records with nothing, and the confirmation names them separately, because every other collection is *replaced* while these are simply *gone*.
- **A client can answer an invite, by text or by email** (TODO §1.6's confirm link; SMS ruled in by the maintainer — "let us have SMS too, for sure") — there is no server, so the answer travels as a message whose body carries a LibrePT deep link the trainer taps once. Three things worth not re-deriving:
  - **A page, not two links in the invite body.** The original sketch put prefilled `mailto:`/`sms:` Confirm links in the invite. That works in an email and fails in a text: an `sms:` URI inside an SMS body is not linkified by most messaging apps, so the SMS leg would have had no working route at all. Both channels carry a plain `https` link, and the reply URI is built on the page at tap time, once the client has chosen a channel.
  - **No new route was needed.** An invite link is already the app's own root with `?evt=`, so the boot decision turns on what the payload IS: an invite means the client is answering, an RSVP means the trainer is collecting one. Inventing `/rsvp` would have stranded links already sent.
  - **The reply names nobody.** It carries `{sessionId, clientId, answer}` — no name, no title, no location. A title like "Post-surgery rehab" would otherwise disclose a medical fact about a named person to every system the message passes through, and a reply crosses a carrier and comes to rest in two message histories. A test greps the encoded payload for each. The invite carries `organizerPhone`, which is what makes a text reply possible at all.

  **The invite leg landed with it**: every invite carries the reply link, and a client with a phone number gets a **Text it** button beside the email one — a text cannot carry the `.ics`, so email keeps the calendar file and the text carries the link. The trainer's number is a third string on `trainerIdentity`, deliberately not validated: numbers are written a dozen ways, the app never dials it, and rejecting a real one is the expensive mistake.
- **Client self-onboarding, end to end** (TODO §1.7/§26, [UC8](use_cases/uc8_client_self_onboarding.md)) — a prospective client opens `/intake` on their own phone, says who they are, ticks consent there, and shares the result; the trainer reads it and accepts. Nothing is typed twice and nothing passes through a server. Both open questions were ruled on by the maintainer:
  - **The submission travels as a FILE, not a link** ("use shares"). `navigator.share({ files })` puts it into the client's own mail or messaging app. Three consequences: nothing sensitive rests in a URL, a carrier's logs or two message histories; the file is a **retainable artifact** carrying the wording version, language and date ticked, which is far better Art. 7(1) evidence than a query parameter; and with no size budget a signature or photo becomes possible later. This **retired §26.2's fragment codec unbuilt**, and §19.2's URL-privacy question does not arise here at all. It declares **both** a media type (`application/vnd.librept.signup+json`) and a distinctive extension, because an Android intent routes on the type, an OS association on the extension, and email frequently relabels the type to `octet-stream` so only the extension survives; the trailing `.json` keeps the file openable where no association exists, which is every desktop and iOS. **The download fallback is permanent, not temporary** — `canShare({files})` is false on every desktop browser and on iOS below 15, and a prospective client standing in a gym cannot be told to come back with a different phone.
  - **Goals and injuries are the client's to offer** ("customer should provide goals and injuries if they decide to do so"). Optional at every level, and a blank field is **absent** from the record rather than stored as `""`, so a trainer can tell "chose not to say" from "not asked yet". Prose over 1000 characters is refused rather than truncated: a sentence that stops mid-clause reads as the client's own words, which is worse than a refusal when it describes an injury someone will program around.
  - **`/intake` is stateless by construction**, not by discipline — its own boot step (`appBoot.bootIntake`), so the client's device gets no database, no seed, no service worker, no first-run agreement, no splash hold, and no write of any kind (`initTheme` included, since it persists the theme). A flag threaded through the trainer's boot would have worked until the day one step was missed, and the failure would be a stranger's phone holding a LibrePT database. Pinned at two tiers: the medium test checks all three storage APIs are empty after a submission, the e2e test checks the boot **decision**, which the medium tier structurally cannot see.
  - **The review dialog is the trust boundary**, and there is deliberately no signature to check — signing needs a key exchange, which needs the server this project does not have. What makes a crafted file acceptable is that a human reads one record before it enters the register. Consent shows as **evidence** (when, which wording, which language) rather than a tick; dedupe is an **offer**, matched on email or phone and never on name, declinable because two people do share a number; an update assigns only what the client supplied, so a skipped box cannot wipe a trainer's note; and declining discards the parsed submission rather than leaving it primed for a later stray tap.
- **The guided walkthrough** (TODO §9.5, with §9.4) — see the entry below; shipped the same day.

### Fixed

It caps to the space under the header and scrolls, using `dvh` rather than `vh` because mobile Safari's `vh` includes the retracted URL bar — exactly the strip a bottom item would hide behind — and `overscroll-behavior: contain` so a flick inside the menu does not scroll the dashboard underneath. Every device that cannot share a file (all desktops, iOS below 15) would have been offered a button that throws. Found by a medium test **written before the wiring**; an after-the-fact browser probe had already reported the page working.

## 2026-08-17 — A guided walkthrough, and the last unbuilt promise is kept

### Added
- **The guided walkthrough ships** (TODO §9.5, with §9.4) — `?demo=walkthrough`, and the splash's own `#splash-walkthrough` button, which was **disabled and marked "soon" until today**: the last control in the app announcing something that did not exist. It explains one step at a time over the real app, with **Back / "Show me" / Next**. Four decisions worth not re-deriving:
  - **One script, not two.** It plays the same [gymFloorTour.js](src/modules/demo/gymFloorTour.js) the automatic demo plays, through the same `performStep` tap. A second script would be a second thing that has to stay true of the app — exactly the drift §23.5 chose a script over a screen recording to avoid — and the demo's e2e replay already keeps this one honest.
  - **The trainer doing the step themselves is what advances it.** Completion is the step's own expectation becoming true, watched on a poll, so it does not care who caused it: no scrim, no backdrop, the app fully tappable underneath. A walkthrough that only advanced on its own buttons would be teaching its own buttons. "Show me" is the escape hatch for someone who cannot find the control, and it withdraws once the step is done, because an offer to do something already done is a control that does nothing.
  - **A step cannot be skipped, and that is the app's constraint rather than a teaching choice** — there is no circuit card before the clipboard is open and no Too Easy button before a card is in focus, so a Next that worked before the step happened would point at nothing.
  - **Back re-explains; it does not undo.** An inverse for every step is an undo stack for a demonstration. A step returned to stays done, so re-reading what Too Easy meant cannot log a second signal — and leaving is one tap that keeps whatever the trainer already did.

  The panel **moves to the top of the screen when it would cover the control it is pointing at**: a guide that hides the thing it is highlighting is worse than no guide on a phone, where there is nowhere else to look. The pure cursor is [domain/walkthrough.js](src/domain/walkthrough.js) (7 `node:test` cases); [tests/e2e/test_walkthrough.py](tests/e2e/test_walkthrough.py) drives the real controls, including a case that pins the caption is **translated copy and not an i18n key** — a missing translation renders the key itself, which is invisible to every other test and is the first thing a trainer would read.

- **The landing page offers three doors instead of two** (TODO §23.5) — watch it drive itself, walk through it with the guide, or poke at the sample gym unguided. The middle one is what a trainer who liked the video but does not want to be dropped into an unfamiliar app actually needs.

## 2026-08-12 — Drive sync works for real users; the counter stops lying; docs come in-app

### Added
- **A withdrawal is recorded, not erased** (TODO §27.7, Art. 7(1)) — §27.4 gave the client a way to withdraw; this gives the trainer somewhere to put it. The only action before was unticking the consent box, which wrote `consentDate`, `formVersion` and `formLang` **blank** — so honouring a withdrawal destroyed the evidence that consent had ever been given, at exactly the moment a supervisory authority would ask for it. Unticking records a `withdrawnDate` and keeps all three. `cloudSync` stays the single "may I process?" flag every caller already reads, rather than gaining a rival some branch would miss; re-ticking drops the withdrawal date, because signing again is a new consent and not an undo. **Never-consented and withdrawn render as different states** in the profile badge and in the client's own Art. 15 export — the subject reading it is the person most entitled to see their withdrawal was acted on. Needed **no schema bump**: `gdprConsent` is an unconstrained object. The archiving dialog was corrected too — it told trainers to *delete the client's records* on withdrawal, conflating Art. 7(3) with Art. 17 and losing the withdrawal record with everything else.

Consent is given by replying **"I CONSENT"** / **"PRIVOLIM"**; withdrawal is replying **"WITHDRAW"** / **"PREKLICUJEM"** to the same message. The symmetry *is* the legal standard — the same reply to the same person is exactly as easy, not merely possible. **No stored trainer contact was needed, and deliberately none was added**: the letter reaches the client *from* the trainer's own address, so reply is already one-tap and survives them changing address. It pins configured-but-not-connected instead — Connect live, and neither Disconnect nor the interval control offered, since either would imply a session this device has never had. ## 2026-08-11 — Two data-subject rights get code; backups any build can open; consent on paper

### Added
- **Erasure, as anonymization rather than deletion** (TODO §17.3, §27.1) — identity is replaced with a pseudonym **derived from the record's own opaque id**, and the execution records stay. That settles §17.3 as **irreversible**: a reversible scheme needs a mapping, and with no server the mapping would live in the database it protects — one file that un-erases everyone. It reaches `clientName` denormalised onto history and planUpdates (the failure [DATA_MODEL §5](docs/DATA_MODEL.md) warns about) and trainer-typed prose no schema marks as identifying. Body-weight history goes with the name — **a body weight measures the person, a session load measures the work**; consent survives as the Art. 7(1) evidence that everything before was lawful. **Two Jane Does is ordinary in a gym**: prose in records the client *owns* is rewritten, text several clients share is left as typed and reported for a human pass. Dialogs identify their target by alias/email/phone/joined/id-tail, never by name. The receipt itemises what erasure cannot reach — the gym calendar (automatic once §1.5 lands), sent mail and SMS, old backup files, and the signed consent form, deliberately **kept**.
- **A register of erasures that survives a restore** (TODO §18.11, §27.3) — yesterday's backup still names an erased client, and §18.7 wants backups restorable indefinitely, so "old files rotate out" is not a defence. Salted id hashes re-anonymise **at import**, before restored data becomes live. The register travels inside backup files and is **unioned**, never replaced — a file is one moment, and adopting its copy would drop every erasure made since. That forced a **fresh salt per entry**, since one install-wide salt cannot be merged across devices; duplicates become unscannable as a side effect, which is strictly better.
- **A per-client export, encrypted** (TODO §27.2) — whitelist-scoped to one client, so handing one client another's health data is structurally hard rather than filtered against. A group session is disclosed as a **size, never a roster**. The trainer's notes **are** disclosed — an assessment about a person is that person's data — and Art. 15(4) redaction is *declared in the file* rather than silently applied. AES-GCM-256 over PBKDF2-SHA-256 (600k), because email is plaintext-at-rest and this is one person's complete health record. **A `mailto:` cannot attach a file on any platform**, so the flow is download → compose → attach yourself, with the passphrase sent by SMS and generated as readable words because it gets dictated over a phone. The client decrypts in an app-menu dialog on their own device — an attachment nobody can open is worse than no export.
- **The trainer-facing privacy doc now describes buttons that exist** (TODO §27.5) — [PRIVACY_FOR_TRAINERS.md §5](docs/PRIVACY_FOR_TRAINERS.md) tabulated four data-subject rights against "what to do in LibrePT" and two had no code behind them. A compliance document naming a button that does not exist is worse than one saying "do this by hand": the trainer discovers the gap while a statutory clock is running.
- **Paper GDPR consent, per client and per language** (TODO §3.5) — a *Data Protection* fieldset with an **editable signed date** and a **versioned letter** (`gdprConsent.formVersion`, a full ISO date: consent is consent to a *wording*, and without the stamp "who is still covered?" is unanswerable after the text changes), delivered by email **and SMS** through the device's own apps. Client documents live per locale in [src/i18n/consent/](src/i18n/consent/index.js) with printable editions in `docs/templates/<lang>/`, and the language is **per client** (`gdprConsent.formLang`) — a Slovenian trainer must be able to send one expat client an English form without switching the whole app. One version spans all languages, because translations state the same promises. The archiving reminder is a **dialog, not a tooltip**: LibrePT stores no signature, so the trainer holding the paper is the whole evidence chain.
- **Backups are written at a stable numbered schema** (TODO §18.7) — the export was a raw `JSON.stringify(getState())`, restorable only by the exact build that produced it. Files now project through `STABLE_SCHEMA` on the same path the star-write fan-out uses, so a file cannot drift from what the store would write. **ONE shape per file**: expand-first staging (TODO §18.4) makes the newest numbered shape a strict superset of every older one, asserted by a test rather than trusted as convention. Files carry `exportedAt` (so a migration deriving dates does not reach for `new Date()` and stamp a two-year-old backup as today) and `buildSha`. The cost — a preview-only field does not reach the file — is stated in the UI as a sentence, not an icon.

- **A confirmation before a restore replaces real data** (TODO §18.7) — a restore *replaces*, which is right (merging two databases needs a common ancestor, which Drive's three-way merge has and a file import does not), but it was silent: a trainer setting up a new phone lost the client they had already entered. The prompt **names what would be lost per collection** — "8 clients, 13 sessions" is a sentence they can weigh, "Are you sure?" is not — and appears only when something is at stake.

Provenance is **never inferred from id shape**; the seeded exercise catalog is an asset, not a stain, and is kept with the reason on its own line rather than in a tooltip.
- **A geometry gate** (TODO §25) — every test asserted semantics and none asserted geometry, so a control running off the screen edge passed the whole gate. [`overflow_scan.py`](agent_tools/overflow_scan.py) sweeps two invariants (nothing past its nearest **clipping ancestor**; nothing clipped inside its own box) across iPhone 14, Galaxy S23 Ultra and desktop, plus one Slovenian pass at the narrowest width, since overflow is a text-length bug. **The mechanic worth knowing**: `body { overflow-x: hidden }` masks the whole class from any root-level `scrollWidth` check, so the check must be geometric and per-element. Router-driven `pushState` walks ~20 routes on one cold boot. No allowlist — intentional clipping opts out in the markup as `data-clip="intentional"`. **Three false positives came first**, and the last is the one to remember: *"wholly outside its box" is benign only OUT OF FLOW* — a parked drawer sits outside on purpose, but in-flow content outside its clipping box has been deleted, not truncated. Exempting it unconditionally reported the wide phone and let the narrow, worse case through.
- **A live Google canary on a bounded stored credential** (TODO §1.5.1) — the hermetic suites pin our request shapes and structurally cannot see Google deprecating an endpoint or reclassifying a scope. **Two attempts at storing no secret came first, and the wall is worth carrying forward**: Workload Identity Federation can only mint a *service account* token, and Google refuses a service account's `appDataFolder` writes for lack of storage quota — so the half of the canary that matters most cannot run keylessly at any price. It carries a real account's refresh token as an Actions secret, bounded by a consent screen set to **In production** (valid until revoked, changed or six months unused, rather than Testing's 7-day expiry) and by production's own two scopes. Deliberately **not** a deploy gate: gating a release on a third party's uptime blocks a deploy for an outage no revert can fix. **The stored secret is not the thing to forbid — leaking it is, and so is a canary reporting confidence it has not earned**: tests enforce no `pull_request_target`, the credential read from `secrets.` and never `vars.` (world-readable, prints in plain text), both *derived* fields masked (`refresh_token` and `client_secret` are what a `set -x` would dump and neither matches the blob byte-for-byte, so neither is masked automatically), and a missing credential fails the run **before checkout** rather than passing green having watched nothing. The scope check moved to [`tokenScopes.live.test.mjs`](tests/live/tokenScopes.live.test.mjs), asking `tokeninfo` what was *actually* granted — strictly better, since it also catches an over-broad grant that would keep every Drive test green while production's narrow `drive.appdata` was broken.

### Changed
- **One numbering axis for record schemas and the migration chain** (TODO §18.14) — the two used small integers for different things (`{2, 3}` versus `1–4` plus `P`), so "schema 3" meant one thing in `recordSchemas.js` and another in `migrationSteps.js`. `LIVE_SCHEMAS` is now `{4, P}` and 4 is the same 4 the chain ends at. **Only one of the two live shapes is durable**: `schema4` is what a backup is written at and what `P` is rebuilt *from*; `schemaP` is what this build reads, disposable, its fields free to change on any commit. `P` is rebuilt whenever the recorded build SHA does not match the running one, and an **absent** marker counts as not matching — "we don't know which build wrote this" resolves to a projection pass, not to a read of fields that may not be there. `"P"` is deliberately not a number: a fraction in stored data reads as a real schema version to anyone inspecting a database.
- **Prose-only commits are exempt from the full gate** — a one-paragraph TODO edit cost a 2m02s run of lint, Node tests, a `pip-audit`, two browser suites and a ZAP scan to verify Markdown none of them can observe. The boundary is **prose**, deliberately not "docs": `docs/SRC_MODULES.md` and the `INDEX` files are parsed by tools, and a mixed code+doc commit is a code commit.

### Fixed
- **The v1→v2 migration dropped `bookings` instead of renaming them.** `bookings` was *renamed* to `sessions` — same records, same shape — and the step dropped them anyway, reasoning that pre-release data was not worth protecting. **That reasoning has expired**: preview instances are demoed on real trainers' devices and backups arrive from every version 1–4, so a database still carrying `bookings` lost its entire schedule. Also restored the per-version chain a previous commit collapsed, and `detectSchemaVersion` now honours any version at or above the floor at face value — reading a stored 4 as "start over" is what re-asked a language question the trainer had already settled.
- **The ellipsis deleted half the edit-mode title bar** (TODO §25.5) — `#session-title-text` is `white-space: nowrap; text-overflow: ellipsis`, **and an ellipsis eats whole ELEMENTS**, so on a 390px phone the edit-mode status chip sat 169px outside the box with no "…" to say anything was missing. It is the only thing on screen distinguishing editing a LIVE session from a future one. **Reordering does not fix that, it chooses the casualty** — moving the chip first made the client name vanish by the same 169px, which the new sweep caught on the next run. The fix is structural: a flex row where the chip and "Editing" declare `flex: 0 0 auto` and only the client name shrinks, with `min-width: 0` doing the work (a flex item's default `min-width: auto` refuses to shrink below its content).
- **Fresh demo data opened on sessions six hours past.** The seed anchored every "today" session with a `min(17, max(3, hour))` clamp that stops following the clock after 17:00, so an evening load opened on a wall of past sessions counting down in negative hours. Slots now follow the real hour and may cross midnight, and each session's `day` bucket is **derived** from the timestamp rather than asserted alongside it, so the two cannot disagree. Separately, a card left open past its start printed a negative countdown: the magnitude is unsigned now and the **label** carries the sense, flipping to "Overdue" via a CSS class the existing 1s ticker toggles rather than re-rendering a card under a trainer's thumb mid-set.
- **A `NaN` star-write target, caught by e2e rather than reasoned about** — `Object.keys(LIVE_SCHEMAS).map(Number)` produced `[4, NaN]` once a schema key stopped being numeric, so every write targeted a `schemaNaN` store that does not exist and 139 of 141 e2e tests failed on a splash timeout. Non-numeric schema keys are threaded, never coerced.

### Added

- **Artifact capture dropped from the gated run** — not tracing, not video, and not screenshots. Any artifact option activates pytest-playwright's per-test recorder around every browser context. Screenshots had survived on the claim that `--screenshot=only-on-failure` is "essentially free at collection time"; measured, that was false — **~40s of a 175s stage (~23%) on a fully green run**, where by definition not one screenshot is written. Escalate on demand instead (`--screenshot=on` on one node id).
- **A failing e2e test now fails the build, with no automatic re-run.** The old serial re-run forgave failures on the theory that xdist port contention produced spurious timeouts; it masked a genuine app-level async race (two uncoordinated `requestAnimationFrame` callbacks stomping each other's result) that went unnoticed for a while. - **`#btn-sync-data`'s handler moved to the module that owns its markup.** Both defects hid behind the same thing: **a stub that hand-duplicates production wiring will agree with itself and disagree with the app.** Mount the real `bootXyz` step, or the test proves only that the test is self-consistent.

### Ruled out by measurement — do not re-derive
- The service worker (disabling registration made it *worse*: 38.3s → 52.7s wall; a `page.route` stub cost 59 failures in 702s because interception routes every request through the Node driver); dev-server throughput (2791 req/s); the TCP listen backlog (`Recv-Q` sampled at 0 throughout); host CPU (peak 2.4 of 16 cores); and CPU governor (`power-saver` costs ~21%, worth clearing, but fixes nothing). ## 2026-07-27 — Data layer: record identity, IndexedDB engine, and schema projections

First steps of the star-write architecture (TODO §18): a data layer that writes every record to
all supported schema versions at once, so moving between app versions loses nothing either way.

### Added
- **Record identity moved to UUIDv7** ([`recordId.js`](src/data/recordId.js)) — 122 bits of
  collision resistance plus lexicographic time-ordering, replacing a 41.4-bit `Math.random`
  generator that carried a 1.38% chance of at least one collision over five years of a very busy
  PT's records. All call sites switched to `newRecordId()`. **Decided (TODO §18.2, closed):**
  identity is the record's own `id` acting as a `lineageId` — no separate old-id→new-id mapping
  table; a set-difference completeness check gives migration the same guarantee a mapping table
  would have, at no extra storage cost.
- **IndexedDB engine, parts 1-3** — [`indexedDb.js`](src/data/indexedDb.js): one database, one
  object store per schema, transactions that resolve on commit, collection + client indexes
  (including a compound `byClientAndCollection` index for per-client queries).
  [`storageDurability.js`](src/data/storageDurability.js): requests eviction-proof storage on boot,
  reports risk by measuring the consequence (quota, `persist()`) rather than sniffing for private
  browsing. [`writeQueue.js`](src/data/writeQueue.js): write-behind persistence — reads stay
  synchronous against in-memory state, writes serialise through a queue. None of this is wired into
  `stateStore` yet (still on localStorage); this is the engine the eventual swap will run on.
- **Schemas exist as data** — [`recordSchemas.js`](src/data/recordSchemas.js) declares the current
  schema's per-collection field shapes, and [`recordProjections.js`](src/data/recordProjections.js)
  projects each live domain object into it, proven against real seed data and the actual object
  literals live writers build (not an idealised model), in `test_record_schemas.py`. This is the
  single-schema half of TODO §18.4's staging guard; the fan-out into an actual IndexedDB bucket and
  the cross-schema half both wait on a second live schema existing.
