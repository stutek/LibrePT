Privacy notice for clients

Your personal trainer gives you this notice before asking for your consent. It says what they record about you, why, who receives it, how long they keep it, and what your rights are (GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Art. 13). The consent letter summarises it.

Who is responsible for your data

The trainer who gave you this notice is responsible for your data: they are the controller (Art. 4(7)). Their name and contact details are in the letter or message that brought you here. Send every question and request to them.

The trainer keeps the records in LibrePT, a free, open-source app that runs on their own device. The makers of LibrePT run no server and receive no copy of your data. They cannot see, change or delete it.

What is recorded about you

  • Identity and contact: your name, and the email address or phone number you give.
  • Training: your goals, session dates, exercises, sets, repetitions, loads, and how each session went.
  • Health: injuries, pain, limits on movement, body weight, and similar notes the trainer needs to train you safely. The GDPR treats health data as a special category (Art. 9), so your explicit consent is asked for.

Nothing is recorded automatically about your device or your browsing. The app has no analytics, no tracking cookies and no advertising.

Why, and on what legal basis

To plan your sessions, adapt your programme over time and train you safely. The legal basis is your explicit consent (Art. 6(1)(a) and Art. 9(2)(a)), given by your reply to the consent letter or by your signature on it.

Do you have to give it

No. No law and no contract requires you to give these details, and you do not have to consent. Without your consent the trainer cannot keep records about you in the app. Without information about your health, they cannot adapt your training to it.

Where it is kept, and who receives it

  • On the trainer's own device, in the browser's storage. There is no LibrePT server.
  • A backup copy in the trainer's own Google Drive, if the trainer turns backup on. It is kept in an area only this app can use. Before the copy leaves the device, the app encrypts it with a password only the trainer knows, so Google stores the file but cannot read it. Google Ireland Limited provides Google Drive in the EU. Google may also keep data in the United States; that transfer relies on the European Commission's adequacy decision for the EU–US Data Privacy Framework (Decision (EU) 2023/1795).
  • An AI assistant, if the trainer uses one to help plan your training. It receives only a copy made by the app: a number, session dates, exercises and sets. The copy holds no name, no contact details, no goals, no notes and no health information. Only the trainer's device can link the number to you, so the GDPR calls the copy pseudonymised (Art. 4(5)), not anonymous. The trainer chooses the AI service, and it may be outside the EU.
  • Messages the trainer sends you — email, SMS, calendar invitations — go through the trainer's own email and phone services, as any message does.
  • Your data is never sold and never shared with advertisers. Nobody else receives it.

How long it is kept

While you train with the trainer, and at most two years after your last session, so your history is there if you come back. Earlier if you ask. The signed consent form, or your reply to the letter, is kept as proof that you consented, also after an erasure (Art. 7(1), Art. 17(3)(e)).

Your rights

At any time and free of charge, you can ask the trainer to:

  • Show you what is held about you and give you a copy in a file a computer can read (Art. 15, Art. 20). The app exports your whole history.
  • Correct anything that is wrong (Art. 16).
  • Erase your data (Art. 17). The app then removes your name, contact details, goals, notes, injuries and body weight from the trainer's device and from the next backup. The training records stay, linked to a number that no longer leads to you.
  • Restrict the processing (Art. 18), for example while you dispute whether the data is correct.
  • Withdraw your consent (Art. 7(3)), in any form and without giving a reason. Withdrawing is as easy as consenting. It stops further processing and does not make the processing before it unlawful.

The trainer answers within one month (Art. 12(3)). No decision about you is made by a machine alone, and you are not profiled (Art. 22).

If you think your data was handled wrongly, you can complain to a data protection authority, above all in the country where you live or work (Art. 77):

  • Slovenia: Informacijski pooblaščenec, Dunajska 22, 1000 Ljubljana, gp.ip@ip-rs.si, www.ip-rs.si
  • Germany: the authority of your federal state, listed by the Datenschutzkonferenz
  • Austria: the Datenschutzbehörde, dsb.gv.at
  • Every other EU and EEA country: the list of authorities kept by the European Data Protection Board
